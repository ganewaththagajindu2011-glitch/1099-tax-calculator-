Quarterly taxes for 1099 contractors in 2026

Freelance clients usually do not withhold taxes from your payments. Estimated tax payments let you pay federal income and self-employment tax during the year as you earn income. A savings target helps with budgeting, but your required payment follows IRS rules.

Who generally needs estimated payments?

You generally need estimated payments if you expect to owe at least $1,000 after withholding and refundable credits and your withholding and credits will be less than the applicable payment threshold. A W-2 job or a spouse’s withholding can affect this test. Use Form 1040-ES to determine your obligation.

Understand the 90% and 100% payment rules

In general, taxpayers can avoid an estimated-tax underpayment penalty by paying at least 90% of current-year tax or 100% of prior-year tax, whichever is smaller, through timely payments and withholding. The prior-year return must cover 12 months. For taxpayers with prior-year adjusted gross income above $150,000 ($75,000 if married filing separately), the prior-year percentage generally rises to 110%.

A safe-harbor payment is not your final tax bill. You can still owe the remaining balance when you file. Timing matters: paying enough at the end of the year does not necessarily fix earlier missed installments.

What if freelance income changes through the year?

Re-estimate when income or expenses change. The annualized income installment method may help align payments with uneven earnings; Form 2210 and its instructions explain the rules. Keep records of payment dates and amounts.

A monthly savings target versus an IRS payment

If your estimated annual tax is $12,000, a simple budget target is $1,000 per month or $3,000 per quarter. Your required payments may differ because of withholding, credits, safe-harbor rules, prior payments, and when income was earned.

How to make a federal estimated payment

  1. Estimate income and eligible business deductions.
  2. Use the current Form 1040-ES worksheet to determine payments.
  3. Use an official IRS payment option, select the correct estimated-tax payment type and tax year, and retain confirmation.
  4. Check state estimated-payment requirements separately.

Use the IRS estimated taxes guidance, Form 1040-ES, and official IRS payment options for current instructions.
