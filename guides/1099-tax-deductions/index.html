Freelancer tax guide · US federal · 2026

1099 tax deductions for freelancers

Eligible business expenses reduce the net profit used to estimate self-employment and federal income tax. A deduction reduces taxable income; it does not reduce your tax bill dollar for dollar. Keep a record of the business purpose, date, amount, and any personal-use allocation.

Common business expenses to review

Software, subscriptions, and professional services

Business software, website hosting, bookkeeping, and professional services can be deductible when ordinary and necessary for your work. Allocate shared personal and business subscriptions reasonably, with supporting records.

Home office

A qualifying area generally must be used regularly and exclusively for business and meet the applicable business-use tests. Eligible taxpayers may use actual expenses or the simplified method, generally $5 per square foot up to 300 square feet. A room occasionally used for work and personal activities generally does not satisfy exclusive use.

Vehicle expenses

Eligible business driving may qualify for the standard mileage method or actual vehicle expenses, subject to method-specific rules. Commuting between home and a regular workplace is generally personal. Keep a mileage log with dates, destinations, business purpose, and distance. Do not claim the standard mileage allowance plus the same vehicle operating costs.

Equipment and supplies

Office supplies and business equipment can qualify, but longer-lived equipment may require depreciation. Expensing elections, business-use percentages, and other rules determine how much you can deduct in a particular year. Do not assume every equipment purchase is fully deductible immediately.

Travel, meals, marketing, and training

Qualifying business travel and advertising can be deductible. Eligible business meals are generally 50% deductible, subject to exceptions. Entertainment is generally nondeductible. Work-related training must meet the applicable rules; training that qualifies you for a new trade or business generally is not a deductible business education expense.

Keep records that support each deduction

  • Save receipts and invoices, with notes about business purpose.
  • Separate personal and business spending where practical.
  • Track mileage and business-use percentages as you go.
  • Reconcile payments, refunds, and reimbursements to avoid double counting.
  • Keep supporting records for the period applicable to your return and assets; the IRS recordkeeping guidance explains retention rules.

How deductions change a tax estimate

For $75,000 of freelance revenue and $10,000 of eligible business expenses, net profit is $65,000. The calculator generally applies regular self-employment tax to 92.35% of that profit, subject to the earnings threshold and Social Security cap. Federal income tax then uses separate adjustments and your filing status.

Keep personal tax deductions separate

The standard deduction, qualified business income deduction, eligible self-employed health insurance, and retirement contributions have separate rules. Do not enter your standard deduction as a business expense: our calculator already applies the basic standard deduction for your filing status. QBI, health insurance adjustments, and retirement contributions are not modeled by this calculator.
