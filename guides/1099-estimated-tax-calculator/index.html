1099 estimated tax calculator for 2026

If you earn freelance, contract, or gig income, a 1099 estimated tax calculator can help you estimate federal income tax and self-employment tax before filing. The goal is not to predict an exact tax return. It is to give you a practical planning number for what you may owe and what you may want to set aside.

What a 1099 estimated tax calculator should help you answer

How much might I owe?

Start with net business profit rather than gross client payments. Eligible business expenses can reduce the profit used in your tax estimate.

How much should I save?

A calculator can turn an annual estimate into a monthly or quarterly savings target. That budgeting target is not automatically the same as an IRS required installment.

Do I have self-employment tax?

The IRS generally requires self-employment tax when net earnings from self-employment are $400 or more. Schedule SE is used to figure the tax.

Do I need estimated payments?

Independent contractors often have no tax withheld from client payments, so estimated payments may be needed during the year. Form 1040-ES provides the official worksheet and rules.

How the estimate works

The calculator begins with your freelance or contractor income and subtracts the business expenses you enter to estimate net business profit. It then estimates regular self-employment tax and federal income tax under the assumptions shown by the calculator. Filing status affects the federal income-tax calculation.

This is intentionally a simplified planning tool. It is most useful when your situation is mainly self-employment income and you want a fast federal estimate. It should not be treated as a completed tax return or a substitute for Form 1040-ES, Schedule SE, or professional advice.

What is included and what is not

The current calculator is designed around a basic 2026 federal estimate. It can use your entered business expenses, filing status, the standard deduction assumptions built into the tool, and regular self-employment tax calculations. It also shows estimated monthly and quarterly savings targets.

Actual taxes can differ because of state or local taxes, W-2 wages, other income, tax credits, itemized deductions, Qualified Business Income rules, retirement contributions, self-employed health-insurance adjustments, Additional Medicare Tax, prior-year safe-harbor rules, or other facts not modeled by the calculator.

1099 estimated tax example

Suppose a freelancer expects $80,000 of client revenue and $15,000 of eligible business expenses. Their starting net business profit estimate is $65,000. A 1099 tax calculator can use that profit, along with filing status and its stated assumptions, to produce a federal tax estimate and a savings target.

The savings target is a budgeting aid. Your required estimated payment can be different because the IRS uses withholding, credits, safe-harbor rules, prior-year tax, and the timing of income when determining underpayment requirements.

Three ways to make your estimate more useful

  1. Use realistic annual income. Re-estimate when contracts, rates, or workload change.
  2. Track eligible business expenses. Review our 1099 tax deductions guide and keep records that support each expense.
  3. Separate a savings estimate from a required payment. Read the 2026 quarterly tax guide before deciding when and how much to pay.
