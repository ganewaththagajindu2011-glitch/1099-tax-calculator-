How much tax will I pay on 1099 income in 2026?

For $50,000 of freelance income with no expenses, this calculator estimates $10,461 in federal income and regular self-employment taxes, leaving about $39,539 after those taxes. This example assumes a single filer and excludes QBI, credits, state taxes, and other adjustments. Your result can be different.

$50,000 in 1099 income: a step-by-step example

  1. Business profit: $50,000. In this example, revenue is $50,000 and deductible business expenses are $0.
  2. Net earnings for SE tax: $46,175. Multiply $50,000 by 92.35%.
  3. Regular self-employment tax: $7,064.78. At this income, $46,175 × 15.3% covers Social Security and Medicare.
  4. Federal taxable income: $30,367.61. Subtract half of regular SE tax and the $16,100 basic standard deduction from $50,000.
  5. Federal income tax: $3,396.11. The first $12,400 is taxed at 10%; the remaining $17,967.61 at 12%.
  6. Total estimate: $10,460.89. Add regular SE tax and federal income tax. That is about 20.9% of business profit under these assumptions.

The calculation uses unrounded intermediate amounts; displayed figures are rounded. The 15.3% SE rate is not your entire tax rate: federal income tax is a separate calculation, and the Social Security component has an annual cap.

What if the same freelancer has $10,000 of business expenses?

With $50,000 revenue and $10,000 of eligible expenses, profit becomes $40,000. This model estimates $5,651.82 in regular SE tax and $2,280.89 in federal income tax: $7,932.71 total. That is $2,528.18 less tax than the no-expense example, not a $10,000 tax reduction.

Income after the entered expenses and estimated taxes is $32,067.29. Spending money solely for a deduction does not make you better off: the expense exceeds the tax reduction in this example. If a cash cost is not deductible, budget for it separately.

How much should a freelancer set aside each month?

Divide a realistic annual estimate by 12 for a monthly savings target. In the $50,000 no-expense example, that is about $872 a month or $2,615 a quarter. Revisit the estimate when revenue, expenses, or household circumstances change.

A savings target is not your required IRS installment. Withholding, prior-year tax, credits and uneven income can change what you need to pay. Use the 2026 estimated-payment deadlines and safe-harbor guide before making payments.

When these examples will not fit your return

These are simplified federal examples, not a refund calculator. They exclude W-2 or spouse income, QBI, tax credits, state/local taxes, retirement and health-insurance adjustments, itemized deductions, Additional Medicare Tax, and alternative minimum tax. They assume an under-65 filer who is not blind and uses the basic standard deduction. Business losses are floored at zero in the estimator. See the methodology and limitations.
