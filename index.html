How to Calculate Your 1099 Self-Employment Tax

This free 1099 tax calculator works as a self employment tax calculator and freelance tax calculator for US independent workers. Start with your net profit: total freelance income minus eligible business expenses. Generally, 92.35% of this profit is subject to self-employment tax.

The self-employment tax formula

(Income − expenses) × 92.35% × 15.3%

The 15.3% rate includes 12.4% Social Security and 2.9% Medicare. For 2026, Social Security applies to the first $184,500 of net self-employment earnings; Medicare has no cap. This calculator applies that cap and treats earnings below $400 as exempt from SE tax.

Then estimate federal income tax

Subtract half of your SE tax and your standard deduction from net profit. We apply progressive 2026 brackets to the remaining income, so only the income in each bracket is taxed at that rate. “Net Taxable Earnings” above means business net profit, before these personal deductions.

Tax Deductions That Lower Your 1099 Tax Liability

Ordinary and necessary business expenses can reduce your taxable profit. Track receipts and claim only the eligible business portion.

  • Home office

    A space used regularly and exclusively for business.

  • Software & subscriptions

    Work tools, hosting, and professional services.

  • Equipment & supplies

    Computers, office supplies, and business gear.

  • Vehicle & mileage

    Qualified business trips, excluding your commute.

  • Marketing & education

    Advertising and training for your existing work.

  • Travel & meals

    Eligible business travel; meals are often 50% deductible.

Some equipment costs must be depreciated. Personal deductions, such as qualifying health insurance or retirement contributions, follow separate rules and are not modeled here.

Frequently Asked Questions (1099 Tax FAQs)

How much should I set aside for 1099 taxes?

Use your estimated total tax divided by 12 or 4 as a starting savings target. The effective rate shown here is estimated federal plus SE tax divided by net business profit. Your actual savings need can change with state taxes, other income, deductions, credits, and withholding.

When do I need to pay quarterly estimated taxes?

You generally need estimated payments if you expect to owe at least $1,000 after withholding and refundable credits. For tax year 2026, the usual due dates are April 15, June 15, and September 15, 2026, and January 15, 2027. Special rules and disaster relief can change deadlines. Required payments depend on IRS safe-harbor or annualized-income rules, not simply one quarter of this estimate.

What is the difference between W-2 and 1099 taxes?

Employers generally withhold income tax from W-2 wages and pay half of Social Security and Medicare taxes. Independent contractors generally handle their own estimated payments and pay both portions through self-employment tax. If you also have W-2 wages, use a calculator that accounts for combined wages and the Social Security cap.

Do I owe taxes if I didn’t receive a 1099 form?

Income can be taxable even when a client does not send you a 1099. Generally, you must pay self-employment tax when net self-employment earnings reach $400. Income-tax filing requirements depend on your overall circumstances; a reporting threshold for a form is not a tax-free allowance.

Which deductions are included in this estimate?

Your entered business expenses, half of regular SE tax, and the 2026 basic standard deduction: $16,100 for Single, $32,200 for Married Filing Jointly, or $24,150 for Head of Household. This estimate excludes itemized deductions, age/blindness additions, QBI, credits, and Additional Medicare Tax. It assumes no other income and no Social Security wages.
